# Second Witness — Brief

## Summary
Second Witness shows the volunteers who run the Global Meteor Network which of their cameras are working alone and which ones the network depends on. It opens on the whole network: the islands of stations that never confirm each other, the stations whose loss costs the most orbits or cuts an island in two, and beside them the cameras that detect meteors every night but that no other camera confirms, so none of their meteors becomes an orbit, though they still count for the network's flux. GMN's Trajectory Viewer already shows one camera's partners; Second Witness shows the whole network, who depends on whom and what a station's loss costs (D21). From there a coordinator can take any camera down and see who loses their only partner and how many orbits a month disappear, or see which installed neighbour could turn its camera to pair with a lonely one. For any camera it says which other cameras its owner should talk to and why, with a message ready to paste on GMN's mailing list, so volunteers who share a sky can find each other. The network is kept month by month for a year, so a camera's page shows when it lost its partner and a country's shows whether it is getting better. Every answer is a Cypher query on an embedded Relate graph that runs in a browser tab, and a coding agent can ask the same over MCP.

## Problem frame
A meteor only gets an orbit when two or more stations see it, so a GMN camera is worth what its partners are worth. More witnesses also make a better orbit: in October 2026 the median velocity uncertainty was 0.311 km/s with two stations, 32% lower with three and 69% lower with five or more. Coordinators write every year that cameras are still waiting for partners and that cameras stop reporting, but nothing shows the network as a network. GMN's own Status page answers a geometric question per point of the map (how many fields of view cover this spot) and flags inactive cameras. It does not show which stations actually confirm each other, which ones run every night with nobody to confirm them, which one is the only partner of another, or what a region loses when one goes dark. Today operators plan overlap by comparing KML files by hand, and a decommissioned camera's cost to its neighbours is invisible until their orbit counts drop.

- D1. ## Impact
- **Persona**: a GMN national coordinator, an operator deciding where the next camera in their country goes, or the volunteer behind a camera that runs with no partner and does not know whose camera could pair with it, like the coordinators who list "lonely cameras" by region in each annual report (D1) and the Saskatoon operator who asked the list how to stop being a lone camera (D10); `sin contactar`
- **Pierde hoy**: "a camera in my region went quiet and I only noticed when my neighbour's orbits dropped": cameras that film meteors every night for nothing, orbits that nobody can confirm, and new cameras pointed by guesswork. Coordinators ask whether a failed camera will stop trajectories from solving (D13), and operators ask why their detections do not pair (D15)
- **La cifra**: in October 2026, 1,431 stations uploaded data and 115 of them detected meteors but shared none with another station, so none of their detections became an orbit; ES000C uploaded all 31 nights with 1,797 detections and zero orbits. Between September and October 67 stations stopped appearing; in September they had been one of the two witnesses of 1,228 orbits, and 629 neighbours lost meteors they used to share with them (NZ002C lost 408). Of the 126,593 orbits of October, 62,267 (49.2%) rest on exactly two stations; HU000B is the second witness of 899. The network is 18 islands that never confirm each other, and one station, US004L, is the only link between 86 stations and the rest of North America. Measured with `research/measure.py`; the figures are in `research/metrics-202610.json`
- **Despues del evento**: offer the monthly country report to the GMN coordinators on their mailing list, as a page they can open without installing anything

## Safety
- **Accion irreversible**: `sin accion irreversible: Second Witness only reads GMN's public data and its own published store; it writes nothing to GMN and contacts no operator; the operator posts the introduction, Second Witness sends nothing. A camera usually sits at a volunteer's home: the page never shows a position finer than GMN's public coordinates file (2 decimals) and moves each one up to 2 km at random, as GMN's meteor map does`

## Demo surface
- **Que ejecuta el jurado**: on the public page, sees the whole network's islands and the stations it depends on, with the cameras that work alone beside them, picks one, opens its link or types a station code or a country, and presses "Take it down" or "Who could turn"
- **Que resultado nuevo sale**: the stations that lose their only partner, the stations left with a single one, the orbits per month that lose their second witness, or the stations it alone was tying to the rest of the network, each with the path of shared meteors that proves it; for a lonely camera, the nearby installed cameras that could pair with it by turning, marked as an estimate; and for any camera, whom its owner should introduce themselves to, with the message to copy; and how a station or a country changed over the last year
- **Que apuesta demuestra**: the network's real shape is in the orbits, not in the fields of view; Spain looks well covered on the map and still rests 94% on two-station orbits (D16). Two cameras whose fields overlap on a map may never confirm a meteor (weather, aim, clocks), and two that look apart may confirm hundreds. Building the graph from who actually saw the same meteor turns a coverage map into a dependency graph that can say what breaks, and crossing it with what each camera uploaded separates a camera that is off from one that works and nobody confirms
- **Donde**: `sin superficie: not built yet; the PLAN decides the public URL`

## Actors
- A1. GMN coordinator or camera operator — looks up a station or a country, asks what depends on it and whom to talk to
- A2. Judge — opens the page or clones the repo and runs a take-down without asking anything
- A3. Coding agent — asks the same question over MCP on behalf of a coordinator

## Requirements
- R1. Given a month of GMN trajectories, I get the graph of stations that saw the same meteor, weighted by how many meteors each pair shared [criterion: Effective use of connected context]
- R2. When I take a station down, I see which stations lose their only partner, which are left with one, how many orbits that month lose their second witness, and for how many it was the third witness that made the orbit more precise [criterion: Usefulness]
- R3. Every answer shows its proof: the shared-meteor count on each edge, a sample of the trajectory IDs behind it, and a link that opens GMN's meteor map on that month filtered to meteors seen by both stations (`https://tammojan.github.io/meteormap/?data=YYYY-MM&stations=A;B`), so a coordinator can check it in GMN's own data and tools [criterion: Clarity]
- R4. I find a station or a country by typing any part of its code or name [criterion: Effective use of connected context]
- R5. A judge opens the public page, or clones the repo and runs one command, and gets a take-down answer without an account, a key or a server [criterion: Completeness]
- R6. The graph is a persistent Relate store, rebuilt from GMN's public files by one documented command; the current month is reloaded every day as a partial slice marked with its cut-off date and GMN's own generation time, and published with the page, and published as a release once the month closes [criterion: Completeness]
- R7. A coding agent asks over MCP with named tools (take down, lonely cameras, country report) and gets the same answer as the page [criterion: Effective use of connected context]
- R8. The fragility of the whole network fits on one screen: its islands, the stations whose loss costs the most orbits or splits an island, the stations with a single partner, and the share of each country's orbits seen by only two stations [criterion: Creativity]
- R9. The README says which Relate mode the store uses and why, what the graph cannot know (a camera that uploads zero detections may face clouds or a broken lens), how to run the first take-down, and that the current month's counts change during the day because GMN rewrites its files several times a day [criterion: Clarity]
- R10. When I open a country, I see whether its stations confirm meteors with any neighbour country and which single station, if any, holds two of its regions together [criterion: Usefulness]
- R11. I see which stations stopped appearing since last month and what each one's neighbours lost; in the current month, a station with no upload for more than 36 h is shown as late and for more than 3 days as dark, UKMON's camera-status thresholds [criterion: Usefulness]
- R12. I see which islands come closest to each other, measured between the cameras of each one [criterion: Creativity]
- R13. The first screen shows the whole network's fragility (R8) and, beside it, lists the cameras that upload detections but have no partner, apart from those that stopped uploading and those that upload zero detections, and says that such a camera still measures meteor flux and that a missing partner can come from aim, calibration, mask or weather, without claiming which (D20) [criterion: Usefulness]
- R14. For a running camera with no partner, I see which nearby installed camera could pair with it by turning, with both cameras' current aim, marked as an estimate [criterion: Creativity]
- R15. A coordinator gets one page per country for the month: islands, cut points, running cameras with no partner, cameras lost since last month and the third-witness count [criterion: Clarity]
- R16. Every number on the page and from the MCP tools comes from a Cypher query on the published store; islands, cut points, same-site groups, moved codes and "could pair" are computed by the loader and read from the store with Cypher, and the README names them (`docs/adr/0004-loader-computes-islands-cut-points-and-geometry.md`) [criterion: Effective use of connected context]
- R17. For a station, I see the stations its owner should talk to, each with its reason (could pair by turning, marked as an estimate; was your only partner and stopped; is your only partner for N orbits), as a list of station codes with their reasons that the operator, or a coordinator who knows the owners, can pass on (not every operator reads the list and only GMN's lead knows every owner, D20), plus an optional message in English to copy to GMN's mailing list that names only station codes and numbers from the store, with the meteor map link of each pair it cites [criterion: Usefulness]
- R18. Every station and every country has its own link that opens its view, and the introduction message carries the station's link [criterion: Clarity]
- R19. I pick two stations and see the shortest chain of stations that share meteors between them, with the shared-meteor count on each link, or why there is none (no partner, or different islands) [criterion: Effective use of connected context]
- R20. For a station, I see its last 12 months: nights uploaded, detections, orbits and its partners with the meteors shared each month, with the month it lost a partner or was left with none marked [criterion: Usefulness]
- R21. For a country, I see month by month over 12 months its share of two-station orbits, its cameras running with no partner, and whether its island joined or split from another [criterion: Creativity]
- R22. For a station running with no partner, late or dark, I see a probable cause read from GMN's own files — clock not synchronized, under half of the night recorded, cloudy, upload failure, decommissioned or old calibration — with the file and night it came from, or "unknown" when GMN published nothing [criterion: Usefulness]
- R23. When I pick a station on the map, a line goes to each of its partners, coloured by how many meteors they shared, with a panel of partner count, nearest and farthest partner [criterion: Clarity]

## Key flows
- F1. **Take it down** — A1 types a station code → picks it → presses "Take it down" → the stations that lose their only partner or drop to one, the orbits per month lost and the ones that lose their third witness, each with its shared-meteor path. (A1; R1, R2, R3, R4)
- F2. **Where is the network fragile** — A1 opens a country → which island it belongs to, which neighbours it confirms with and how far the nearest other island is, its share of two-station orbits, the stations whose loss costs the most orbits or splits the island, the ones with a single partner and the ones running with none; the country view is the monthly report, shared by its link and printable. (A1; R8, R10, R12, R13, R15, R18)
- F3. **Judge check** — A2 opens the page or runs the one command against the published store → a take-down answer appears → A2 opens the store and runs the README's Cypher query. (A2; R5, R6, R9, R16)
- F4. **Agent asks** — A3 calls the MCP tool with a station code → the same take-down answer as the page. (A3; R7)
- F5. **What changed since last month** — A1 opens the change view → the stations that stopped appearing, with the meteors each neighbour lost. (A1; R11)
- F6. **Who could turn** — A1 picks a running camera with no partner → the installed cameras nearby, with their current aim and the turn that would cross both fields at 100 km, marked as an estimate. (A1; R14)
- F7. **Country report** — merged into F2. (A1; R15)
- F8. **Introduce** — A1 opens a station → sees "Talk to" with each station and its reason → copies the message, which carries the station's link, and posts it themselves on https://globalmeteornetwork.groups.io/g/main. (A1; R17, R18, R14, R11, R2)

## Acceptance examples
- AE1. **Covers R1.** Given the committed October 2026 fixture, when the judge queries the edge between two stations, then its weight equals the number of trajectories in `traj_summary_monthly_202610.txt` whose participating stations include both.
- AE2. **Covers R2.** Given a station that is the only partner of another station in the month, when the user takes it down, then that other station is listed as losing its only partner.
- AE3. **Covers R2, R3.** Given HU000B in the October 2026 store, when the user takes it down, then the orbits lost equal the count of two-station trajectories that include HU000B, and at least one trajectory ID is shown for each affected partner.
- AE4. **Covers R4.** Given the committed October 2026 fixture, when the user types "BR", then the Brazilian stations are listed.
- AE5. **Covers R5, R6.** Given a fresh clone with no network and no keys, when the judge runs the documented command, then a take-down answer is printed from the committed October 2026 fixture.
- AE6. **Covers R7.** Given the MCP server running on the committed October 2026 fixture, when an agent asks about HU000B, then the answer equals the page's answer for HU000B, verified with a recorded agent run and not only a direct tool call.
- AE7. **Covers R9, R13.** Given a station in GMN's station list, when the user looks it up, then Second Witness says "no data uploaded" if it is absent from the month's index, "running, no partner" if it uploaded detections but shares no meteor, and "uploaded, zero detections" otherwise, without claiming why.
- AE8. **Covers R8, R10.** Given the October 2026 store, when the user takes US004L down, then 86 stations are shown as cut off from the rest of their island, and the answer says they still confirm meteors among themselves.
- AE9. **Covers R8.** Given the October 2026 store, when the user opens Spain, then its share of orbits seen by only two stations equals the count over `traj_summary_monthly_202610.txt` of Spanish trajectories with exactly two participating stations.
- AE10. **Covers R11.** Given the September and October 2026 stores, when the user opens the change view, then 67 stations are listed as stopped, and NZ002C is shown as having lost 408 shared meteors.

## Key decisions
- Global Meteor Network station pairs — chosen over the Backfocus adapter-chain solver, a DarkSky ordinance-precedent finder and an Astronomical League program planner, because GMN's helped group is counted in its own reports, its coordinators state the pain in writing (D1, D2), and the graph comes straight out of its open data. settled: user-approved
- Pairs measured from orbits, not inferred from fields of view — chosen over geometric overlap from the KML files, because that is what GMN's Status already does (D4, D11), because coverage and confirmation diverge (D16), and because a measured pair is the only one that produced an orbit. settled: user-approved
- The take-down question is the product — chosen over "where should I point my new camera", because that answer is a geometric prediction and this one is measured; it reuses the blast-radius pattern already built in hindsight (`knowledge/hackathons/datahub-agent-hackathon/README.md`). settled: user-approved
- A camera with no partner is shown as "running, no partner", never as wasted: its detections still feed GMN's flux, and unmatched detections can also come from calibration, a mask, weather or non-meteors (D20), so nights and detections are context and the page shows no "wasted detections" figure. settled: user-approved
- The pitch opens on GMN's real network and its own operators' words (D18–D21), not on the chokepoint idea, which other entries also use (D22). settled: user-approved
- Up to date every day: GMN rewrites the current month's file several times a day (November 2026 to date: 72 MB, modified 2026-11-25 09:54 UTC), so a scheduled GitHub Actions job reloads the current month daily as a partial slice and publishes the page data and a `gmn-latest.rl` on GitHub Pages; closed months are published as GitHub Release assets and never committed, so git history does not grow with the store (`docs/adr/0007-closed-months-as-release-assets.md`) — chosen over closed months only and over the page fetching GMN's daily files live (which breaks R16 and depends on GMN in front of the judge) (`docs/adr/0006-daily-partial-month-published-not-committed.md`). settled: user-approved
- Second Witness complements GMN's meteor map instead of redrawing it: the meteor map shows meteor by meteor who saw what; Second Witness shows the network, who depends on whom and what a loss costs, and every pair links to the meteor map filtered to that pair and month as its visual proof (R3). No map of our own; the README credits Tammo Jan Dijkema's meteor map and Milan Kalina's meteorview. No code is copied from either (the meteor map is GPL-3.0) (`docs/adr/0005-fov-from-gmn-kml-and-proof-links-to-meteor-map.md`). settled: user-approved
- "Who could turn" crosses GMN's own field-of-view polygons at 100 km (`kml_fov/<CODE>-100km.kml`, CC BY), the files the meteor map and meteorview draw; they are a current snapshot with no history, so the answer carries the date the polygons were fetched. Aim numbers and every past month come from the dated pointing history in `GMN_station_coordinates_public.json`. settled: user-approved
- The whole network's fragility as the first screen (islands and the stations whose loss costs the most or cuts an island), with running cameras with no partner listed beside it — chosen on 2026-11-25 over opening on the lonely cameras alone, because GMN's Trajectory Viewer already shows one camera's partners (D21) and no GMN tool shows the whole network or what a loss costs; the lonely cameras stay on the same screen because they are the finding a coordinator can act on today. settled: user-approved
- "Who could turn" — chosen over "where to put a new camera", because turning an installed camera costs nothing and uses the aim GMN already publishes; it stays marked as an estimate because it is geometry, not a measured pair. settled: user-approved
- Twelve calendar months, one slice per month, the latest as the default view — chosen over two months and over a single day, because a trend shows when a camera was left alone and whether a country is improving, comparing consecutive months still shows who stopped, and a day leaves stations unpaired by weather (71 single-partner stations in the one-day file downloaded on 2026-11-21, against 28 in all of October 2026). Each month stays its own slice, so a whole year never hides a camera that already went dark. settled: user-approved
- Sources are the monthly trajectories, the station coordinates and the per-night upload index, loaded into the published store — chosen over GMN's Datasette platform, which was down (D8), and over reading GMN live from the browser, because the demo must not depend on GMN being up. settled: user-approved
- The questions are Cypher queries on the store — chosen over computing them in code and keeping Relate as storage, because the criterion measures how the product uses the graph; the scripts in `research/` only produced the evidence for this brief and are not part of the system. settled: user-approved
- No LLM inside the product; the agent is the user's, over MCP — chosen over a built-in assistant, because the answers are deterministic and a model adds nothing a number does not already say. settled: user-approved
- Runs in the browser on Relate's WebAssembly build, with the same store usable from the CLI and MCP — chosen over a hosted server, because judges must run it "without asking you anything" and the DataHub entry did best when it could be checked without installing anything (`knowledge/hackathons/datahub-agent-hackathon/README.md`). settled: user-approved
- Introductions by station code, posted by the operator — chosen over an opt-in contact directory, because GMN publishes no operator identity, a directory needs a server and personal data (against R5 and Safety), and GMN's mailing list is where its people already meet and where its founder asked them to coordinate (D14). settled: user-approved
- The country view is the country report — chosen over a separate report page, because both showed the same queries. settled: user-approved
- GMN as the only source — chosen over CAMS, EDMOND and FRIPON, because only GMN publishes, openly and every few hours, which stations saw each orbit (D17); UKMON's cameras are already in it. settled: user-approved
- Live Cypher for every question, with islands, cut points, same-site groups, moved codes and "could pair" precomputed by the loader into the store — chosen over all-live Cypher, because Relate 1.2.0 has no graph algorithms and a cut-point query that excludes a station ran over 10 min, while take-down, chain and history run in 2–40 ms (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, S2–S6). settled: user-approved
- The witness chain uses Relate's `shortestPath` — confirmed on October 2026: DE0001 to HU000B in 5 stations, 30 ms; a Spanish island station to Germany returns no chain (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, S3). settled: user-approved
- Name: Second Witness — a meteor needs a second witness to get an orbit. settled: user-approved
- Closed months as GitHub Release assets, with a fixed October 2026 fixture in the repo for the offline clone and the tests — chosen over committing the store every month, which grows git history by the whole file each time (13–33 MB) and leaves the scheduled jobs to be disabled after 60 days without a commit (`docs/adr/0007-closed-months-as-release-assets.md`, 2026-11-25). settled: user-approved
- Three features come from the web review of 2026-11-25 (D23, D24): a probable cause for a lonely or dark camera from GMN's nightly summary and weblog status, partner lines on the map as in ADS-B Exchange's MLAT map, and last night's pairs from GMN's daily file; all three are should, below the twelve must units. A placement map for a new camera, a penalty score that recovers, Blitzortung-style effectivity and a weather overlay stay as later ideas. settled: user-approved

## Scope boundaries
### Deferred
- Where to put a new camera — needs an estimate of the pairs a camera that does not exist yet would make; "Who could turn" is the base it would build on
- More than 12 months — an older month is one more slice, but the demo does not need it
- Other networks (EDMOND's yearly CSV, FRIPON's releases) — another format and a yearly cadence; a pair is a pair, so they could feed the same graph later
### Outside this product
- Orbit quality, meteor showers and any science on the meteors themselves — those are properties of the trajectory, not of who saw it; the orbit uncertainty is used only to weigh a third witness
- Changing anything in GMN or in a station's software — Second Witness reads public data; acting on its answer is the coordinator's call
- Telling a cloudy night from a broken lens — both upload zero detections, so the index cannot hold that difference
- Storing operators' names or contacts, or sending messages — GMN publishes stations by code only; the operator decides whether to post the introduction

## Assumptions
- A station listed twice in one trajectory row counts once, and station codes are matched exactly, never as substrings (the meteor map counts a repeated station twice and filters by substring); rows are keyed by their trajectory ID (`20261124193429_0tBbG`).
- GMN's monthly trajectory file keeps its column layout, with "Participating stations" as the last column. If it changes, the build command fails loudly instead of building a wrong graph. This holds for the 12 months loaded; GMN served the November 2025 monthly file and the Spanish per-night index of 2024-11-15 and 2025-11-15 on 2026-11-23.
- The store keeps, per month, each pair's shared-meteor count, a few trajectory IDs per pair as proof (R3) and per-station counts (nights, detections, orbits, two-station orbits), not the trajectory rows (about 100 MB a month). A synthetic year of that is 33.2 MB as a `.rl` (13.2 MB without trajectory IDs), under the 50 MB budget (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, S5).
- The fourth field of each row in the per-night upload index is the number of meteors RMS detected that night (ES0001 shows 0 and ES0005 shows 118 on 2026-10-20). Confirmed in GMN's own status page, which builds the night's stack image name from it (`<ID>_<stamp>_stack_<N>_meteors.jpg`, `globalmeteornetwork.org/status/`); the third field is unused there. When a station uploads more than one folder in a night, the folder with the most detections counts.
- A station's position and aim are its latest entry in the coordinates file dated on or before the last day of the month.
- Station codes are not fixed: an old code can be reused at a new site, and one camera can alternate two codes by season (FR000U from November to March, BE000J from April to October) — https://globalmeteornetwork.groups.io/g/main/message/14811 · https://globalmeteornetwork.groups.io/g/main/message/14955. A code also stays the same when its camera is turned or moved within the same country (Mark McIntyre, https://globalmeteornetwork.groups.io/g/main/message/16514), so a station's pointing is read per month, not once: `GMN_station_coordinates_public.json` keeps a dated history per station (ES001G: one entry, 2025-06-22, azimuth 127.6°, elevation 36.0°), while the `kml_fov` polygons are only the current pointing. A station that stopped appearing is marked "possibly moved" instead of "stopped" when a code that started that month sits within a few metres of it; the distance is fixed with the same-site threshold in the architecture spike.
- "Who could turn" crosses the two fields of view at 100 km, the height GMN recommends for comparing coverage (D3). It is an estimate, not a pair that produced an orbit.
- Two-station trajectories are a fair proxy for "orbits that depend on a station": a third station might have seen the meteor without making the solution, and that is not counted.
- A cut point measures connection, not lost orbits: the 86 stations behind US004L keep confirming each other. A country's share of two-station orbits also reflects how many cameras it has and how they are aimed, not only fragility.
- Coordinators would use a per-station take-down answer. Nobody at GMN has been asked; the mailing list (https://globalmeteornetwork.groups.io/g/main) is where to ask, and its archive is public; the questions are drafted in `docs/outreach.md`.
- Cameras at the same site are separate witnesses (D20) and a partner is any station a camera shared an orbit with; no pair is dropped by distance. The solver's real rule is in WesternMeteorPyLib, the library GMN's pipeline runs: a candidate pair is rejected when its stations are under 5 km or over 600 km apart (`wmpl/Trajectory/CorrelateEngine.py`, `min_station_dist = 5.0`, `max_station_dist = 600.0`), then the fields of view are checked at 50–115 km; the 5 km minimum is checked only when a new pair is seeded, so cameras under 5 km apart can still end in one solution through merging or when a station is added to an existing trajectory (no distance check there, `CorrelateEngine.py` L1193-1310). Mark McIntyre confirmed the 5 km and 600 km limits and the merge path on the list (#16531, 2026-11-25), and added that the convergence angle must be over 3 degrees, with velocity and altitude limits too; those depend on each meteor's geometry, so "could pair" does not model them and stays an estimate. The data agree: in October 2026, 7,423 of the 62,267 two-station orbits (12%) have their stations under 50 km apart (1,090 under 25 km, e.g. UA0008–UA0009 at 17 km); the 16 under 1 km by the public 2-decimal coordinates point to stale or rounded positions, not to a broken rule. "Could pair" only proposes cameras 5–600 km apart. `site` only groups co-located cameras for display.
- The participating-stations column lists only the stations the solver kept (`AggregateAndPlot.py` drops `ignore_station`), so it undercounts who saw each meteor; the page says so where it shows a pair's count.
- Operators read GMN's mailing list, so a message that names two station codes reaches the owner of the other one. Partly contradicted: not every operator is on the list and only GMN's lead has the full owner list (D20), though two neighbours did meet by code on the list (D19). Country coordinators, and UKMON for about 90% of UK cameras, are the fallback.
